What this is. Eleven subjects for the SSC track, in two halves, cut down to the part that is actually asked. The recall half comes first because it is the part worth having in one place: the eight classical dances, the folk dances of every state and union territory, the festivals and fairs, Ancient and Medieval India, the three new criminal laws, the Himalayas, and Centre–State relations.

The maths half is written as questions, not as a formula sheet. Reading a formula again cannot tell you whether you knew it, so every formula in algebra, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, height and distance, and profit with mixture is set as a question with the four options an SSC paper would give. The answer is not on the screen until you have committed to one, and committing locks the question — a formula you were still deciding about while the answer was visible has not been tested. The explanation that then opens states the formula in full and names which wrong option is the tempting one. Each wrong option is a formula that actually gets confused with the right one: the total surface where the curved surface was asked, the sector where the segment was asked, a − b times a² − ab + b² where the sign inside flips the other way.

The geometry carries drawn figures. One drawing per configuration, in the same lettering every time it is used, so a reader who has learnt the figure does not have to read it again. Nothing is fetched to draw them; they are written into the page.

The three new criminal laws are quoted by number. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam are asked by section number — which section is murder, not what is murder — so every section here carries its number, the old IPC, CrPC or Evidence Act number it replaces, and the punishment. The section numbers were checked against the bare Acts and against published mapping tables rather than written from memory.

Where the popular answer is wrong, the page says so. Chhau is not one state's dance. Garba and Dandiya are not the same thing. Sedition was not simply renamed. Every one of those sits in a Careful box instead of being smoothed over, because the wrong version is the one the question is built to catch.

What is firm and what moves. Dates of enactment, section numbers and the entries of the Seventh Schedule are firm. Heights of peaks, lengths of ranges and the counts of List entries move by small amounts between sources; where they do, the figure carries the version the standard textbooks use, and the note says that it moves.
